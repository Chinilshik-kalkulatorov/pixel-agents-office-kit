# Owner's redesign brief — the checklist the new office must satisfy

The owner reviewed the 24x18 office and ordered a targeted redesign, not a rebuild. His words, condensed into
checkable requirements. The new layout is `mid/v2` (20x16 = 320 tiles); the previous one is `mid/mine` (24x18 = 432).

1. **Left working zone — preserve.** The left top room (CTO office) must not be touched unless there is an objective
   reason. The lower-left room with **8 computers** keeps its concept and its number of workstations. Only spacing,
   furniture and small interior details may be improved. It is one of the best parts of the old design.
2. **Right working zone — cut hard.** It was too big. Two computers on the right had to go, the wall had to move
   inward, and the empty distance had to disappear. The right side must be visibly smaller, not just visually tighter.
3. **Cafe on the right — keep, but adapt.** Do not destroy it. Shrink distances, make the furniture more compact,
   keep the feeling of a real cafe, keep the sofa and the lounge idea if possible, drop anything that eats space
   without function.
4. **Central horizontal hall — remove entirely.** The old full-width corridor was a useless strip. Not a cosmetic
   improvement: it must be gone, distances between rooms shortened, zones pulled together, circulation compact and
   natural.
5. **New entrance logic.** A person enters roughly at the centre and lands immediately in a compact entrance zone:
   a sofa on one side, the receptionist with a computer on the other. **Between the sofa and the reception desk there
   must be a normal passage** so the person can walk left into the working zone, and there must be an obvious way
   right as well. It must not feel like a long empty corridor.
6. **Sofa and table in the entrance.** A sofa like the cafe's, placed logically relative to the entrance, with a small
   table behind it. Do not invent odd furniture just to make it smaller; the main thing is not to block the passage.
7. **Decorative plants in the old central hall — remove.** Anything that eats useful space without real function goes.
   Greenery may stay in the office generally, but as part of the design, never as an obstacle to movement.
8. **Central (CEO) office — significantly smaller.** It did not justify its area. Shrink it a lot, keep it feeling
   like a real executive office, and do not leave large empty zones around it.
9. **Left office (CTO) — light touch only.** Tidy the plants, improve furniture placement, remove visual noise, make it
   read more expensive. Keep its idea and structure.
10. **Right office — full redesign.** It was the weakest room. Rework the plan, furniture, workstation, plants, decor,
    spacing and composition so it reads as a properly designed room in the office's style.

**Overriding goal:** a compact, dense, logical office with no useless space. Left preserved, right much smaller,
horizontal hall gone, excess passages and voids cut, central office much smaller, left office lightly polished,
right office fully redesigned.

The owner also said: do not follow the letter of the instructions where it hurts the design — he described intent and
functional logic; judge as a professional space architect, and prefer a better solution as long as it keeps the intent
(less empty space, tighter plan, better passages, higher functionality).
